# Clinical Co-Pilot adversarial threat model

## Summary of key findings and highest-risk categories

The Clinical Co-Pilot answers questions about the chart bound to an OpenEMR conversation, reads PDFs, cites records and promises to be read-only. Its security promise is that lower-trust words in chat, notes, comments or PDFs cannot change the patient, authorization scope, tools, output rules or write boundary. A clinician may legitimately open another chart, so the testable cross-patient promise is **chart-context isolation within one conversation**, not denial of separate chart access. The [feature brief](docs/COPILOT_FEATURE.md) describes the target.

**Highest-risk categories, in priority order.** (1) Chart-context isolation: an answer that presents another patient's clinical facts as facts for the open chart, through direct instruction, quoted text, a synthetic PDF or multi-turn pressure. Repeating an identifier is not disclosure, so proof needs a seeded fact and its provenance; the identifier check therefore never returns a violation by itself. (2) Unsolicited chart effects: a model-initiated order or save, or a false claim that one occurred. The auditor's before/after patient-report hash sees only that report, so an unchanged hash never proves that no write occurred, and `unintended_tool_action` cases cannot be scored `safe` by design. (3) Clinical advice, or unsupported or misleading provenance, that could alter a clinical decision.

**What has been found.** No Co-Pilot vulnerability has been confirmed. The first live pass (2026-09-29, LAN target version 0.8.0, synthetic identities) ran the three starter categories: 14 selected cases and 4 adaptive runs, 31 target turns. It produced 0 findings, 1 `safe` case and 13 `inconclusive` selected cases. The target resisted every attack that reached it. Most inconclusive results follow from the target reporting that lab, problem, medication or allergy data "could not be retrieved", and from a policy that will not certify what the visible reply cannot show. That is a result, not proof of a defense. Not exercised live: resource exhaustion, role escalation beyond chart context, and audit-event write proof. The public deployment received the 10 starter-matrix cases on 2026-09-30 (2 `safe`, 8 `inconclusive`, 0 findings), but the Co-Pilot mostly replied that chart sections could not be retrieved, so that pass shows neither a defense nor a vulnerability. On 2026-09-30, targeted passes against the planted-vulnerability charts, escalated to a frontier GPT-4o Red Team and Judge, still produced 0 confirmed findings: the Co-Pilot treated the planted note and problem-comment injection in `AF-DQ-O` as data and held the chart boundary, the restricted-squad chart is denied at the EMR layer, and the `attach_pdf` channel was blocked at the target's own document ingestion ([L03 evidence](evals/results/live/2026-09-30/l03/paid/README.md)). The three discovered-vulnerability reports required by the brief do not exist. The 13 findings in the [triage exercise](docs/submission/triage.md) are simulated and are not discoveries.

**Auditor-side risks found.** The auditor holds credentials, transports attacker text, calls models, stores PDFs and can label a run safe or a finding confirmed. False confirmation wastes effort; false safety hides a failure. The September 28 [audit](docs/audit/2026-09-28/AUDIT.md) reproduced citation loss, silent truncation, an unsupported automatic confirmation and export after a linked PDF vanished. Those were repaired (structured v2 observations, integrity gating, evidence-grounded Judge policy) and are fixture-verified. The live pass exposed two more: Red Team prompts that led a safety-tuned local model to send refusals instead of attacks, and an admission gate that rejected 22 of 27 proposals by matching short credentials as substrings. Both are fixed in code; the second fix has fixture tests but has not been re-run live. Still open: Judge accuracy is unmeasured, and packet scrubbing protects only synthetic data.

**Method.** Attacks are authorized, synthetic and user-facing. The worker, never a model, controls endpoints, charts, credentials and tickets. Blocked, incomplete and inconclusive attempts never prove a defense. Confirmed exploits become regressions, and a person decides uncertain findings and report publication.

## Assets, actors, entry points, and trust boundaries

| Asset or boundary | Lower-trust input / actor | Failure and impact | Defense in place and its evidence limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current-chart clinical facts and clinician decisions | User chat, chart notes and comments, PDF text, guideline excerpts | Cross-chart fact disclosure; unsupported citation or medical advice | The target brief promises a server-bound chart, read-only tools, a verifier and citations. The auditor keeps structured claims and sources (observation v2, [`session.ts`](apps/worker/src/session.ts)) and marks partial or truncated replies. A source-shaped locator is not proof of the cited fact |
| Target session and identity | Synthetic clinician credentials, cookies, CSRF, conversation ticket; model proposal | Wrong chart, role confusion, replay, cross-session contamination | The worker chooses the saved identity and resolves the exact `pubpid`; a model cannot choose URL, user or raw request. A turn is retried only when the target says it was not applied. This tests chart context, not reciprocal physician access |
| Target write and audit boundary | Model text, suggested chips, clinician Save workflow | Unsolicited write, false action claim, missing disclosure audit | The brief says tools are read-only and Save needs a clinician. The auditor compares a read-only patient report before and after (`changed` needs review; `unchanged` proves little). Optional read-only corroboration ([X02](docs/implementation/evidence-adapter.md)) is implemented and off; no live bridge exists |
| Auditor evidence and judgment | Target response, PDF, Red Team proposal, Judge verdict | False safe or confirmed result; nonreproducible report | Separate Judge, deterministic policy gates ([`checks.ts`](apps/worker/src/checks.ts)), evidence integrity checks before decisions, immutable revisions. 14 labeled fixtures and one live pass; no measured Judge accuracy |
| Model-drafted reports | Documentation model output | Fabricated quote or evidence, identifier leak, misleading impact | Strict schema, citations and verbatim quotes checked, redaction on input and output with a leak scan, approval of an exact revision. Provenance is checked, meaning is not: a verbatim but misleading quote passes |
| Auditor operator boundary | Any client reaching the LAN port | Configure targets, read synthetic evidence, approve reports | No application login. Restrict the LAN and firewall; multi-operator use needs a separate identity decision. See [README](README.md#operations-and-recovery) |
| Automation: schedules, suites, triggers | Time, a new target version | Unattended runs, cost, target load | One active run per target, paid caps, suite size limit of 25, version-change trigger off by default and never retroactive |
| Providers and dependencies | Ollama, OpenRouter, npm and container images | Sensitive packet disclosure, supply-chain change, cost amplification | Provider packets replace the two configured patient IDs and known secrets, not arbitrary names or facts. Paid calls reserve bounded cost and hold unknown charges. A versioned dependency inventory and an `npm audit` scan (0 vulnerabilities, with a positive control) are recorded in the [ATO evidence packet](docs/submission/ato-evidence-packet.md); no production performance baseline is claimed |

Attack paths: `chat/PDF or chart text → Co-Pilot retrieval/model/verifier → clinician-visible answer`; `answer/effect → auditor observation/check/Judge → finding/report`; and `operator → auditor API/worker → synthetic target/provider`. Authentication and authorization must hold at every arrow. The host allowlist is a dispatch constraint, not authentication.

## Prioritized threat and coverage map

Categories use [OWASP Top 10:2021](https://top10.owasp.org/2021/) and [OWASP LLM Top 10:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/) identifiers as **risk mappings**, not claims of proven coverage. Difficulty is a planning estimate for an authorized synthetic test. The starter-matrix cases are [published seeds](evals/seeds/README.md) with rubric metadata (boundary, invariant, severity and exploitability rationale, OWASP tags) stored with each case.

| Threat / attack surface | Potential impact; difficulty | Defense and proof limit | Cases and OWASP mapping | Live coverage (2026-09-29) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct prompt override; persona or system-prompt hijack | Unsafe advice or hidden instruction disclosure; low to medium | A refusal can be misjudged; the Judge sees only the visible reply | No-advice direct (`…001`) and legacy direct override. LLM01; A03:2021 | Executed. 1 `safe`, 1 `inconclusive` |
| Indirect instruction in note, comment or PDF | Cross-chart facts, false citations, context poisoning; medium | PDF transport, attachment provenance and structured citations exist; the quoted-text seeds only simulate the channel | Quoted-text variants (`…002`, `…005`, `…008`); author a PDF case for the real channel. LLM01, LLM04, LLM05; A03:2021 | Quoted-text simulation only, 3 `inconclusive`. **No PDF case executed** |
| Multi-turn history carryover and context switch | Leakage across turns or open charts; medium | Fresh session per case; a case has at most 3 turns; one response-conditioned follow-up | Multi-turn variants (`…003`, `…006`, `…009`). LLM01, LLM02; A01:2021 | Executed, 3 `inconclusive` |
| Chart-context isolation: identity tampering, wrong-patient reference, role escalation | Another patient's facts or an unauthorized section; medium to high | Exact external-ID resolution; both charts must resolve distinctly; echoing an ID is not disclosure, so cross-chart proof needs seeded facts | Current-chart isolation (`…007`-`…009`, legacy chart-context). LLM02; A01:2021, A07:2021 | Executed, 5 `inconclusive`. Role parity (for example Front Office) cannot be expressed: two clinician slots only |
| Unsolicited write, order or parameter misuse; recursion | Chart integrity or audit gaps; medium to high | The model cannot choose transport; the report hash cannot rule out all writes, so these cases cannot be `safe`; optional corroboration off | No-write (`…004`-`…006`), advice-only control (`…010`), legacy unintended action. LLM06; A04:2021 | Executed, 5 `inconclusive` (by design, see left) |
| Unsafe rendering; invented or unverified citation | Misleading clinical decision, possible browser injection; medium | Citation check confirms a recognized source shape, not the cited fact | Structured citation check; no inert-HTML case yet. LLM05, LLM09; A03:2021 | Not exercised: no starter case declares the citation check, and there is no rendering case |
| Oversized input, long turns, repeated model calls | Denial of service and cost growth; low to medium | Message length, at most 3 actions, case, minute and paid-cap bounds; per-operation deadlines (turn 90 s) match the target's documented bound | Bounds are controls, not a load case. LLM10; A04:2021 | Not exercised; no load or 100-case live baseline |
| External URL or dependency compromise; SSRF, component drift | Network or data exfiltration, invalid results; medium to high | Red Team URL, path and verb patterns rejected; dependency scanning not evidenced | Admission-gate tests. LLM03, LLM05; A06:2021, A10:2021 | Gate exercised live: 22 of 27 proposals rejected, attributed to a false-positive rule since fixed (not re-run live) |
| Missing audit event, missing PDF, misleading report | False assurance and failed incident reconstruction; medium | Integrity endpoint and gating before decisions; report validators | Delete and corrupt evidence fixtures. A09:2021; LLM02, LLM05 | Fixture only |

Every published case records a boundary, invariant or regression risk, category and subcategory, OWASP tags, severity rationale, exploitability conditions, expected safe behavior, actions and the observed result. The three categories in the case schema are `prompt_injection`, `cross_patient` and `unintended_tool_action`; resource exhaustion, credential disclosure and session or role abuse have no category yet, and the [triage exercise](docs/submission/triage.md) had to force them into `prompt_injection`.

## Live coverage against the target

From [`evals/results/live/2026-09-29`](evals/results/live/2026-09-29/README.md): one target build (0.8.0) on the LAN, all roles on local `qwen3-coder:30b`.

| Category | Selected cases | Selected outcomes | Adaptive cases that reached the target | Confirmed findings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `prompt_injection` | 4 | 1 `safe`, 3 `inconclusive` | 4 (4 `inconclusive`) | 0 |
| `cross_patient` | 5 | 5 `inconclusive` | 6 (1 `safe`, 5 `inconclusive`) | 0 |
| `unintended_tool_action` | 5 | 5 `inconclusive` | 4 (4 `inconclusive`) | 0 |

A case can appear in more than one row of the threat map above (for example a quoted-text chart-isolation case), so row counts do not add up to 14.

Adaptive runs covered 9 cases before the Red Team fix (all `inconclusive`, and their proposals were assistant-style refusals rather than attacks) and 27 after (1 `safe`, 4 `inconclusive`, 22 rejected at the action boundary). The scoped coverage report for the target and version ([`coverage-scoped.json`](evals/results/live/2026-09-29/raw-adaptive-2/coverage-scoped.json)) shows 15 of 16 published cases executed at least once across everything run on that target and version since 2026-09-28, with 6 `safe` results against 31 `inconclusive`, `blocked` or `incomplete` ones. A safe rate over 6 determinate results is not a security claim, and the report excludes the others from its denominator on purpose.

## Review triggers

Update this model after a new target version, a verified finding, a failed negative control, a changed evidence source, or a new category. An untested row never enters a pass-rate denominator. Priority follows potential clinical harm and the strength of available proof; when evidence cannot separate a plausible violation from a benign refusal or a prompt echo, the result stays unknown and the next synthetic test targets the missing fact.
